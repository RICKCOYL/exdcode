01 / UX DESIGN & ACCESSIBILITY

Duro Sweden.

Making wallpaper shopping more accessible, intuitive and enjoyable.

ROLE UX / Experience Designer
FOCUS Accessibility & Usability
PROJECT TYPE E-commerce Redesign
01 / INTRODUCTION

Overview

A beautiful wallpaper catalogue deserves an equally thoughtful shopping experience.

Duro Sweden offers a wide range of wallpapers, but the existing website made it difficult for users to explore patterns, compare options and feel confident in their choices.

As an Experience Design student, I worked through the UX process, from user research and information structure to interaction design and UI improvements.

The goal was to create a clearer, more inclusive shopping experience by improving navigation, product discovery and accessibility in line with WCAG principles.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

Too many choices. Not enough clarity.

Buying wallpaper is a visual and considered decision. Users need to explore styles, narrow their options and understand product details before making a purchase.

01

Limited filtering

Restrictive filter options made it difficult to narrow down the catalogue and explore multiple preferences.

02

Unclear navigation

Inconsistent hierarchy and structure made it harder for users to understand where they were and what to do next.

03

Accessibility barriers

Low contrast, unclear focus states and difficult keyboard interactions created unnecessary friction.

03 / OBJECTIVES

The Goals

Create a shopping journey that feels effortless, inclusive and easy to navigate.

  • Improve the accessibility of key pages and interactions using WCAG guidance.
  • Make filtering and product discovery more flexible and intuitive.
  • Strengthen information hierarchy and navigation clarity.
  • Help users compare wallpapers and make more confident decisions.
04 / USER RESEARCH

Understanding the people behind the choices.

I conducted semi-structured interviews with people who had experience buying home decor online or exploring wallpapers.

The conversations focused on how people search, filter, compare and evaluate products, as well as situations where navigation or readability becomes difficult.

Key research findings

Three insights that guided the redesign.

FINDING 01

Users need more control over filtering.

Restrictive filtering and difficult keyboard navigation made it harder to narrow down results efficiently.

FINDING 02

Clear structure creates confidence.

Users sometimes struggled to understand their position within the catalogue and its hierarchy.

FINDING 03

Accessibility affects the entire experience.

Low contrast, inconsistent headings and unclear focus states made browsing more demanding.

05 / DESIGN PROCESS

From insights to better interactions.

The research highlighted opportunities to simplify the browsing experience without taking attention away from the wallpapers themselves.

01

More flexible filtering

Checkbox-based filters allow users to select multiple options, while visible filter chips make active selections easier to understand and remove.

02

Clearer information hierarchy

More consistent layouts, headings and labels help users scan pages and understand the catalogue.

03

Accessible interaction patterns

Stronger contrast, visible focus states and more meaningful labels support a wider range of users and navigation methods.

06 / FINAL DESIGN

A calmer way to explore.

The redesigned interface puts product discovery first, with clearer navigation, more accessible components and a consistent visual structure.

01 / 03 — Homepage

07 / TESTING & ITERATION

Testing the experience.

I tested an interactive prototype using realistic browsing tasks, such as finding wallpapers for a particular room and narrowing results with filters.

I observed navigation choices, hesitation, backtracking and difficulties interpreting the interface. The findings informed further refinements to labels, feedback and product information.

THE PROBLEM

Filtering felt restrictive.

Users could not select multiple filtering options, making it harder to explore combinations of preferences.

THE SOLUTION

Flexible filters with visible feedback.

I introduced checkbox-based filters and removable chips so users could combine options and clearly see their active selections.

08 / OUTCOME

The Outcome

A more thoughtful shopping experience, designed around clarity and inclusion.

The final concept brings together more flexible filtering, clearer product information and improved accessibility patterns.

Usability testing helped identify remaining friction and refine the interaction design. The redesign provides a foundation for easier product discovery and more confident decision-making.

The potential business value lies in reducing browsing friction, supporting more customers and strengthening trust in the brand. These benefits would need to be validated after implementation.

09 / REFLECTION

What I Learned

Accessibility and usability should work together.

This project reinforced the importance of considering accessibility from the beginning of the design process rather than treating it as a final checklist.

I also learned how small interaction details, such as clear filter states, consistent hierarchy and visible feedback, can make a meaningful difference to the overall experience.
